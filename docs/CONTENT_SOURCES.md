# BiteWise Content Sources

This document is the editorial source map for the app's educational bite,
sting, and infestation guides. It records sources used to check symptom
descriptions, self-care language, prevention advice, and escalation cues. Links
were checked on 2026-08-30.

## Medical-review requirement

BiteWise offers general education and possible matches; it does not diagnose a
bite, sting, infestation, allergy, infection, or disease. Appearance alone is
often nonspecific, and a user photograph cannot establish the responsible
species. Every medical claim, self-care instruction, medicine reference,
timeframe, and red-flag rule must be reviewed by a qualified clinician before a
production release and after any material content change. Product copy should
tell users to seek local emergency care for trouble breathing, swelling of the
face, mouth, tongue, or throat, fainting, confusion, or other signs of a severe
reaction. Localize emergency numbers and treatment advice for each launch
region.

Do not describe a match as certain or label an unknown insect as "poisonous."
Use "possible match," distinguish venomous stings from bites, and direct users
to a clinician, pharmacist, poison center, public-health authority, or licensed
pest professional when appropriate.

## Shared safety references

- [NHS: Insect bites and stings](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insect-bites-and-stings/) — general self-care, infection warning signs, urgent review, and emergency allergic-reaction cues.
- [American Academy of Dermatology: Bug bites and stings](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/injured-skin/bites) — dermatologist-reviewed bite and sting care and guidance on when to seek care.
- [MedlinePlus: Insect bites and stings](https://medlineplus.gov/insectbitesandstings.html) — U.S. National Library of Medicine overview of prevention, allergic reactions, and escalation.
- [CDC/NIOSH: Protecting yourself from stinging insects](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2010-117/) — first aid and severe-reaction precautions for bees, wasps, hornets, and fire ants.

## Guide-specific sources

| Guide | Primary references | Editorial use and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mosquito | [CDC: About mosquito bites](https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/about/about-mosquito-bites.html); [CDC: Preventing mosquito bites](https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/prevention/index.html) | Typical local reactions, basic care, infection cues, mosquito-borne illness context, and EPA-registered repellent guidance. Do not imply every mosquito carries disease. |
| Flea | [CDC: About fleas](https://www.cdc.gov/fleas/about/index.html); [CDC: About flea-borne typhus](https://www.cdc.gov/typhus/about/murine.html) | Itching/irritation, animal and environmental context, and uncommon disease warning signs. Avoid diagnosing a flea bite from a skin pattern alone. |
| Bed bug | [CDC: About bed bugs](https://www.cdc.gov/bed-bugs/about/index.html); [CDC: Caring for patients with bed bug bites](https://www.cdc.gov/bed-bugs/hcp/clinical-care/index.html) | Variable reactions, line/cluster patterns as non-diagnostic clues, signs of infestation, minimal symptomatic care, and secondary-infection risk. Confirmation depends on finding and identifying the insect, not the bite alone. |
| Tick attachment | [CDC: What to do after a tick bite](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/after-a-tick-bite/index.html) | Prompt removal with clean fine-tipped tweezers, cleaning, unsafe removal methods to avoid, and follow-up for rash or fever. Never base treatment decisions on the app's visual match. |
| Spider or another skin issue | [CDC/NIOSH: Venomous spiders at work](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/outdoor-workers/about/venomous-spiders.html); [AAD: Bug bites and stings](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/injured-skin/bites) | First aid and urgent symptoms. Emphasize that many skin problems are incorrectly attributed to spiders and that a photo of a lesion alone cannot confirm a spider bite. |
| Biting midge / no-see-um | [University of Arizona Cooperative Extension: Something's biting me, but I can't see it](https://extension.arizona.edu/publication/somethings-biting-me-i-cant-see-it); [University of Florida IFAS: Biting midges](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN626) | Size, habitat, painful or itchy welts, bite prevention, and local-reaction care. Disease risks vary greatly by species and geography; do not generalize livestock or overseas transmission to all U.S. bites. |
| Black fly | [Washington State Department of Health: Biting flies](https://doh.wa.gov/community-and-environment/pests/biting-flies); [Illinois Department of Public Health: Biting flies](https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/environmental-health-protection/structural-pest-control/biting-flies) | Pain, itch, swelling, bite prevention, infection monitoring, and allergic-reaction cues. Keep claims geographically scoped. |
| Horse fly | [Washington State Department of Health: Biting flies](https://doh.wa.gov/community-and-environment/pests/biting-flies); [CDC: About tularemia](https://www.cdc.gov/tularemia/) | Painful bites, allergy and infection monitoring, and context for biting-fly exposure. CDC specifically identifies tick and deer-fly transmission of tularemia; do not rewrite that as proof that every horse-fly bite transmits it. |
| Stable fly | [Illinois Department of Public Health: Biting flies](https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/environmental-health-protection/structural-pest-control/biting-flies); [University of Florida IFAS: Stable fly](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN1114) | Recognition, common ankle/leg exposure, painful bites, and prevention. Use shared safety guidance for any unexpected systemic or severe reaction. |
| Chigger | [Texas A&M AgriLife Extension: Chiggers](https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu/library/landscaping/chiggers/); [CDC: About scrub typhus](https://www.cdc.gov/typhus/about/scrub.html) | Typical placement, intense itching, secondary-infection prevention, and the fact that chiggers do not burrow into skin. Scrub typhus messaging must retain CDC's travel/geographic context and must not imply ordinary U.S. chigger bites transmit it. |
| Fire ant | [Mississippi State University Extension: The fire ant sting](https://www.extension.msstate.edu/insects/fire-ants/the-fire-ant-sting); [CDC/NIOSH: Protecting yourself from stinging insects](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2010-117/) | Burning pain, multiple stings, later sterile white pustules, allergy risk, removing attached ants, and urgent warning signs. Do not tell users to open or drain pustules. |
| Honey bee | [CDC/NIOSH: Protecting yourself from stinging insects](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2010-117/); [MedlinePlus: Bee, wasp, hornet, or yellow jacket sting](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002847.htm) | Sting first aid, possible retained stinger, local reactions, anaphylaxis, and multiple-sting escalation. Medicine advice must remain label-directed and clinician-reviewed. |
| Yellowjacket | [CDC/NIOSH: Protecting yourself from stinging insects](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2010-117/); [MedlinePlus: Bee, wasp, hornet, or yellow jacket sting](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002847.htm) | Venomous sting, repeated-sting possibility, local care, allergy warning signs, and emergency escalation. Do not imply a retained stinger is expected. |
| Head louse | [CDC: About head lice](https://www.cdc.gov/lice/about/head-lice.html); [CDC: Treatment of head lice](https://www.cdc.gov/lice/treatment/index.html) | Diagnosis by finding a live crawling louse, transmission, treatment, retreatment, and household measures. Avoid stigma: infestation is not caused by poor cleanliness. Never recommend pesticides, foggers, or home remedies outside authoritative guidance. |

## Editorial controls

1. Record the reviewing clinician, review date, applicable region, and source
   revision date for every production content release.
2. Re-check source links and clinical guidance at least annually and whenever a
   public-health agency publishes a relevant update.
3. Keep regional disease risks separate from common local skin reactions.
4. Treat community posts and uploaded photos as personal experiences, not
   clinical evidence. Never promote a community identification as definitive.
5. Keep image credits and licenses synchronized with
   [`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
